Nabd: the whole clinic day on one screen

How a front desk juggling three doctors, a paper book and WhatsApp got one live view of the day, so empty slots get filled and no-shows get caught early.

The problem

The schedule existed. No one could see it.

Small clinics run their day across a paper book, each doctor's phone calendar and a WhatsApp group. Booking a caller meant checking three places. Free time and unconfirmed bookings stayed invisible until it was too late.

Research

Every tool shows a calendar. None shows the day.

Jane App, Cliniko and SimplePractice are built around clinical records and priced per practitioner ($45–59 a month for one). Queue tools like WaitWell handle walk-ins but not bookings. None shows a small clinic's whole day, booked and walk-in, with risk marked.

The insight

The pain wasn't booking. It was not seeing.

Design direction: the whole clinic day on one line. Every screen answers who is next, who might not come, and where the free time is.

Make risk visible

Unconfirmed bookings are drawn dashed and listed in "Needs a reminder", with one-tap WhatsApp.

Make free time visible

Gaps of 15 minutes or more show as open slots you can book straight from the timeline.

Seat walk-ins fast

"Add walk-in" places them in the first gap and says where they went.

No dead ends

When a time is taken, the error offers the next free one.

The design

One timeline, one row per doctor, one red line for now.

Built on Untitled UI with a teal brand. Status lives in shape as well as color: dashed for not confirmed, dashed gray for walk-ins, solid for confirmed.

Booking: taken times stay visible and struck through. A conflict blocks the booking and offers the next free time.

Patients, tested with a six-word name.

Profile with a missed-visit warning.

No result: says what to try next.

Empty clinic: import from Excel or add the first patient.
