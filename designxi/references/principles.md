# Design Principles Reference

Read this file only when the user asks for the rationale behind a design decision, or when you need to justify a recommendation with theoretical grounding.

---

## UX Process & Thinking

1. **Design for behavior, not features** *(The Design of Everyday Things — Don Norman)*
   Users don't read — they scan, guess, and click. Design for how people actually behave, not how you wish they would.

2. **5 users reveal 85% of problems** *(Handbook of Usability Testing)*
   Don't wait for perfect research. Test early, test often. 5 interviews catches the key patterns.

3. **Lo-fi first, always** *(Sketching User Experiences)*
   A sketch that's wrong costs nothing. A coded screen that's wrong costs everything.

4. **Research is not optional** *(Just Enough Research — Erika Hall)*
   Even one hour of structured research beats a week of assumption-based design.

5. **Lean over perfect** *(Lean UX — Jeff Gothelf / Getting Real — Basecamp)*
   Ship something real and learn from it. A finished 80% solution beats a perfect 0% solution.

---

## Visual Design

6. **Typography is 95% of design** *(Refactoring UI — Adam Wathan)*
   Get the type scale and hierarchy right first. Color and decoration come last.

7. **Hierarchy before decoration** *(101 Dos & Don'ts of UI Design)*
   Visual weight, size, and position communicate meaning. Never use color as the only differentiator.

8. **White space is design** *(Zen of White Space — UXPin)*
   Negative space gives elements room to breathe and be understood.

9. **Consistency builds trust** *(Principles of Visual Consistency — UXPin)*
   Every inconsistency is a micro-confusion. A consistent system feels professional and trustworthy.

---

## User Understanding

10. **Name users, don't describe them** *(Interviewing Users — Steve Portigal)*
    "Layla, 28, marketing coordinator" is more powerful than "millennials who use mobile apps."

11. **Mental models shape expectations** *(100 Things Every Designer Needs to Know About People)*
    Design that fights mental models loses. Design that leverages them wins.

12. **Emotions drive decisions** *(Seductive Interaction Design)*
    Users need to feel good using your product. Emotion is not decoration.

---

## Product Delivery

13. **Microinteractions make the product feel alive** *(Microinteractions — Dan Saffer)*
    The difference between polished and dead is in the details — button press, loading states, transitions.

14. **Accessibility is not optional** *(WCAG 2.1 AA)*
    A design that excludes 15–20% of users is a failed design.

15. **Information Architecture determines success** *(Information Architecture — Morville & Rosenfeld)*
    If users can't find it, the design failed — regardless of how beautiful it is.

16. **Business requirements shape design constraints** *(Business Thinking for Designers)*
    Every design decision has a business consequence. BRD + FRD prevent that disconnect.

17. **Requirements must be testable** *(The UX Book — Rex Hartson)*
    "The app should be fast" is not a requirement. "Load time < 2s on 4G" is.
